### A1-01-R3 cold-review correction

Cold review proved that a parent `spawnSync` maxBuffer overflow sends `SIGTERM` to the wrapper process only. Because the gate child is a detached POSIX group leader, the earlier wrapper could die before cleaning that group. The runner now installs non-reentrant `SIGTERM` and `SIGINT` handlers and uses one shared first-latch-wins cleanup path:

- Deadline latches exit 124; external signals latch `128 + signal` (143 or 130).
- The winning latch clears the deadline, sends group/direct-child `SIGTERM`, ignores later close and signal events, always attempts `SIGKILL` after exactly 2000 ms, then settles once.
- `ESRCH` remains benign; any other TERM/KILL error overrides the latched result with exit 70.
- Listener/timer cleanup occurs once at settlement, so repeated external signals cannot re-enter or replace the first result.

Runner tests were hardened so every process-group case receives leader PID/PGID through a bounded readiness-file handshake and always attempts `kill(-pgid, SIGKILL)` plus wrapper/leader/descendant cleanup in `finally`. The simple deadline test now follows that rule. A new real parent `spawnSync` test uses `maxBuffer:1024` and a noisy child; it reproduces `ENOBUFS`, waits through wrapper cleanup, and proves neither leader nor SIGTERM-ignoring descendant survives. Separate tests prove deadline-first remains 124 after a later SIGINT and external SIGINT is non-reentrant and deterministically returns 130.

Final cold-review-revision checks: runner suite 7/7 passed in 10.97 s, A1 suite 8/8 passed in 12.95 s, format/lint checked 66 files, typecheck and diff-check passed, and no orphan/hung process remained. No manifest/full gate/ops/stage/commit/push operation occurred.

### A1-01-R3 second cold-review correction

The second cold review injected `EPERM` into both negative-PGID kill calls and proved that setting `process.exitCode = 70` alone was not bounded: the live detached child and inherited capture descriptors kept the wrapper open. The runner now pipes and forwards child stdout/stderr byte-for-byte. At the final 2000 ms grace boundary it always attempts group `SIGKILL`; if either group signal had a non-`ESRCH` failure, it removes listeners and timers, unpipes and destroys the forwarding streams, and explicitly exits 70. Ordinary success, child failure, signal, timeout 124, and first-latch paths remain orderly `process.exitCode` settlements.

A deterministic public/injected regression uses the same captured `spawnSync` shape as `validate-release` (`encoding: 'utf8'`, 20 MiB `maxBuffer`), an infinite child, and an import hook that throws `EPERM` only for group TERM/KILL. Before the correction the parent hit its 6-second watchdog; afterward the wrapper exits 70 at approximately deadline plus 2 seconds while the fixture confirms the child is still live before unconditional external cleanup. The maxBuffer regression now completes a valid nonzero PID/PGID readiness handshake before assertions, retries partial JSON reads, and every group fixture retains `finally` cleanup. Forwarding ignores parent-side `EPIPE`, allowing the wrapper's external-signal cleanup latch to complete after `spawnSync` closes its over-limit capture pipe.
